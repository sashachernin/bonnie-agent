Put Every Pet on One Sitter Schedule

Multi-pet owners turn scattered care details into one timed checklist a sitter can actually follow

The idea

Pet owners leaving several animals with a sitter need every meal, walk, medicine, and exception handled at the right time. Sell a proposed $15 browser-based planner that turns pet-by-pet details into one chronological trip schedule, a one-page emergency sheet, and printable daily checklists. The owner pays once and receives files that work offline; this is organizational help, not veterinary guidance.

A customer example

Hypothetically, Lena is leaving two cats and a dog for four days. She finds the product while searching Etsy for multi-pet sitter instructions, enters each animal's routine, then sees that one cat's 7 p.m. medicine overlaps the dog's walk. She adjusts the order, pays $15, and downloads a four-day packet with timed tasks, locations, quantities, checkboxes, and emergency contacts. Her sitter works down one daily page instead of cross-referencing three profiles, and the purchase pays the business.

Who pays, and for what

The initial buyer is a US owner of two or more pets preparing for a friend, relative, or paid sitter. The same first version serves a one-pet household with several medicines or an involved routine, but not care requiring clinical judgment.

The American Pet Products Association reports 94 million US pet-owning households and says 70% of Gen Z pet owners have at least two pets. The handoff problem is repeated whenever owners travel or change sitters. One owner produced 3.5 pages of food, medicine, walking, and emergency notes; a sitter separately reported owners repeatedly leaving medicine without directions. An Etsy instruction pack has 36 item reviews and a shop with 16,100 sales. These signals establish the job and paid template behavior, not willingness to pay $15 for automatic scheduling.

What the AI agent would build

Version one is a mobile-friendly form, schedule preview, conflict screen, checkout, and download page. The owner adds pets, timed and flexible tasks, quantities copied from existing instructions, supply locations, contacts, and trip dates. Code expands routines across the trip, flags blank doses and overlapping tasks, and produces letter-size and A4 PDFs plus a phone-friendly HTML file. Data stays in the browser; a small backend validates payment and never receives pet details.

The coding agent implements recurrence logic, accessible print layouts, local encrypted saves, and tests for daylight-saving changes and trips crossing midnight. The hardest risk is making flexible routines readable without implying that software has verified a dose. Version one excludes reminders, sitter accounts, cloud sharing, veterinary recommendations, and emergency decision-making.

Launch and ongoing maintenance

The owner arranges Etsy and Stripe accounts, static hosting, privacy terms, and a support inbox. Allow 35–50 build hours and 10–14 owner hours for example packets, listing images, and plain-language review. Ongoing work should take 6–10 hours monthly for browser tests, support, examples, and listing updates; unusual medical schedules receive a refund, not bespoke setup.

For the first ten users, publish an original Etsy digital listing against the observed searches “pet sitter instructions” and “multi-pet sitter instructions.” Etsy currently shows more than 1,000 sitter-instruction items, including listings with hundreds or thousands of reviews, so demand and severe competition are both visible. The entry page must show the decisive difference before purchase: three pet profiles becoming one timed day. Etsy permits sellers' original digital downloads and charges $0.20 per listing plus 6.5% per sale; search placement is unverified. The fallback is 30 permission-based messages to independent sitters with a free sample they can pass to clients, with no assumed response.

Why now

No new rule or technology created this need. The present entry case is a growing multi-pet audience and current purchase behavior, not an unmet-market claim: APPA reports that Gen Z is both the fastest-growing pet-owner group and especially likely to own multiple pets, while the current Etsy category contains 1,000-plus products and heavily reviewed listings. A dated marketplace is useful now because the product can be tested where buyers already search.

What exists today

JournalsWithDragons sells a 30-page fillable pack for $1.99 and has 36 item reviews; it is the strongest low-price substitute. Plan for Paw sells an $8.99 four-page printable and fillable pack. Hearthline Studio sells a $12, 25-sheet multi-pet binder. Free documents and a shared note also work.

Those options are cheaper, polished, and better for owners who want a permanent reference binder. This product earns consideration only by compiling all animals into the order a sitter acts, repeating the correct tasks for every trip day, and surfacing collisions or blanks before printing. Owners already describe long, cross-category notes, while sitters describe consequential omissions. That supports the organization benefit, but whether it is worth $15 remains unverified.

How it makes money

The proposal is $15 per trip packet, including unlimited edits for 30 days. Sixty-seven sales produce $1,005 revenue. At Etsy's verified $0.20 listing and 6.5% transaction fees, that is about $926 before payment processing, optional ads, hosting, tax, and refunds; payment processing varies by seller country. Estimate hosting, email, and monitoring at $30–$70 monthly. Revenue is not profit.

Conservatively, 6,700 qualified listing or site visits at an assumed 1% purchase rate yield 67 sales; optimistically, 1,675 visits at 4% do. Traffic and conversion are assumptions. Budget 10–16 owner hours monthly for two listing experiments, one useful example article, support, and maintenance, plus an assumed five minutes per buyer. Continued growth needs species- and situation-specific examples and sitter referrals, not free traffic assumptions. The large pet-owner count bounds the audience but does not prove that this visit volume is attainable.

The riskiest assumption

The killing belief is that multi-pet owners will pay $15 for a compiled timeline when attractive blank templates cost $1–$12. In one week, recruit 25 owners through 40 permission-based sitter contacts; qualified owners must have at least two pets, a trip within 60 days, and three or more daily care tasks. Manually turn the first 15 completed routines into the real packet, then show the $15 price before delivery. Pass if ten use the packet to review a real handoff, six identify a collision or missing detail, and four make a written purchase commitment at $15. Fail if ten complete the task and none commits; fewer than ten completions is an inconclusive channel test. Ask purchasers after their trip whether the sitter used the checklists; delivered value remains unresolved in week one.

The part I would argue against

A sceptic would call this an expensive form generator in an Etsy price war. They would be right that a Google document is adequate for careful owners, 1,000-plus marketplace results make attention costly, and the $1.99 leader already includes a multi-pet page and care tracker. The positive case survives only as a test because separate owner and sitter reports show that information volume and omissions affect actual care, while reviewed purchases show people already pay to structure the handoff. Abandon if ten qualified owners review a real generated schedule and none commits, or if fewer than six find a concrete conflict or omission.
