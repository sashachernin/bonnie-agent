## The idea

Musicians flying to a performance need to know whether their instrument can ride overhead, needs its own seat, or must be checked. Build a free, source-dated checker that combines case dimensions, weight, fare, operating airline, and each flight segment into a printable plan. It earns disclosed affiliate commission when a traveler buys an eligible hard case; the airline remains authoritative.

## A customer example

Hypothetically, Leo is flying American with a guitar in a 46-by-18-by-7-inch case. He finds a page for “American Airlines guitar carry on,” enters the case, fare, and flight, and sees that cabin space is first-come, an extra seat costs the applicable adult fare, and a checked instrument needs a hard case for American's limited damage liability. He saves the source-linked gate card and follows a retailer link to compare hard cases. B&H, not Leo, could pay the site 2% if he buys.

## Who pays, and for what

The initial user is a US musician flying with a guitar, violin, viola, or cello; the same first version serves students and hobbyists with those instruments. It excludes cargo, orchestral tours, codeshares whose operating carrier cannot be identified, and any guarantee of bin space.

The need is episodic but consequential. A June 2026 discussion about a musician denied cabin carriage drew travelers reporting both trouble and successful guitar trips ([discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/SouthwestAirlines/comments/1ugubtt/southwest_airlines_bans_local_musician_from/)); the American Federation of Musicians tells travelers to carry both the federal rule and airline policy ([travel kit](https://www.afm.org/what-we-are-doing/travel-resources/afm-travel-kit/)). The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 170,400 US musician and singer jobs and says they travel frequently, but does not say how many fly with instruments ([profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Entertainment-and-Sports/Musicians-and-singers.htm)). Retailers pay only when a referred purchase qualifies.

## What the AI agent would build

Version one has an instrument-and-case form, four US-airline rule pages, a segment checklist, source excerpts with checked dates, and a printable gate card. A small database stores carrier, cabin, extra-seat, checked-bag, case, liability, aircraft caveat, and source fields. Rules run deterministically; no account or artificial intelligence is needed.

The coding agent builds validation, affiliate disclosure, analytics, change alerts, and fixtures for common cases. The hardest risk is resolving operating carrier and aircraft-dependent space without implying certainty. Version one excludes bookings, fare scraping, insurance advice, live inventory, and international law.

## Launch and ongoing maintenance

The owner arranges hosting, analytics, affiliate approval, and terms. Estimate 40–55 build hours, then 8–12 monthly hours to recheck policies, four for corrections, and 15–20 for source-led pages and outreach. The agent can flag changed text; the owner must interpret it.

For the first ten users, send 40 permission-based invitations to touring musicians and music teachers with public business contacts, offering to check one real upcoming trip. Search is the scalable channel: current results for “airline musical instrument policy comparison” and “American Airlines guitar carry on” are general articles and airline pages, not a case-and-itinerary test. Exact carrier/instrument pages can answer those queries, although ranking is unverified. Community-promotion permission was not verified; direct outreach is the fallback, not assumed access.

## Why now

The US rule is not new: covered airlines must accept a small instrument in the cabin when it fits safely and space exists at boarding ([law](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A49+section%3A41724+edition%3Aprelim%29)). What is current is visible enforcement anxiety: the June discussion and 2026 airline-comparison pages show travelers still reconciling general rights with carrier limits. The entry route is a personalized, dated answer, not a claim that guidance is absent.

## What exists today

The [US Department of Transportation guide](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/air-travel-musical-instruments) and [American Federation of Musicians travel kit](https://www.afm.org/what-we-are-doing/travel-resources/afm-travel-kit/) are free and authoritative; they are better on rights and disputes. [Travel Vient's 2026 comparison](https://travelvient.com/guides/best-airline-for-checked-instruments/) and [NRG Luthier's guitar report](https://www.guitarradesmontable.com/en/politicas-aerolineas-guitarra-avion.html) are free, broad comparisons. American's own page is free and says a second seat costs the adult fare, while checked instruments use standard bag fees ([policy](https://www.aa.com/web/i18n/travel-info/baggage/specialty-and-sports.html)).

The decisive reason to use the entrant is that it applies the traveler’s actual case and operating segments, then produces one dated card; substitutes require manual reconciliation. That advantage is unverified with users, and incumbents are stronger for explanation.

## How it makes money

Access is free. The proposal is disclosed links to suitable hard cases. B&H's official terms state a 2% base commission, exclude cancelled or returned orders, and require approval ([terms](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/FrameWork/affiliates/BH-Affiliate-Terms-and-conditions.pdf)). At an assumed $250 eligible order, 25% outbound-click rate, and 2% merchant conversion, 40,000 qualified visits yield $1,000: 40,000 × 25% × 2% × $250 × 2%. An optimistic 35% click and 4% conversion needs about 14,286 visits. Returns and unavailable products increase the requirement.

Hosting and monitoring are estimated below $60 monthly. At milestone traffic, allow 20–30 owner hours for content/acquisition, 10–15 for verification, and five for support and partner work. Revenue is not profit. Growth comes from more carrier/instrument query pages and return trips, not assumed free traffic; the traffic burden makes this a validation-first business.

## The riskiest assumption

The killing belief is that personalized policy checks generate relevant case-shopping clicks rather than one quick answer. In one week, recruit 20 musicians with a flight planned within 90 days from 80 permission-based invitations. Manually deliver the proposed card, with neutral case links where appropriate. Pass if 12 submit complete trip and case details, eight use the card, and four click a case comparison; fail if 12 complete checks and fewer than two click. Fewer than 12 completed checks is an inconclusive channel test. Ask after travel whether they used the card; repeat demand remains unresolved.

## What I rejected

- An emergency substitute-teacher packet failed standalone value because the search found abundant editable printables but no supported reason to choose another.
- A food-truck catering quote tool failed the business case because current free calculators and a dedicated toolkit already cover costs, labor, travel, margin, and quote output without an evidenced acquisition advantage.

## The part I would argue against

A sceptic would say musicians can read the free federal, union, airline, and comparison pages, while only travelers who need a new case can generate revenue. They would be right: at 2%, the site needs tens of thousands of qualified visits, and a stale or overconfident answer could destroy trust. The case still warrants a manual test because travelers report real boarding conflict, policies vary by carrier, and the exact-case check removes concrete reconciliation work. Abandon if fewer than two of 12 completed checks produce a relevant shopping click, or if more than one card needs specialist correction.

The prior benchmark is **Buy One Calculator for Every Exam** (`2026-09-23-afternoon-exam-calculator-match.md`). Test the calculator matcher first because its annual buyer pool and verified 5% commission are stronger. Move this idea ahead only if four of 12 musicians click a relevant case and at least one buys, or if the calculator test produces no purchase from 12 qualified shoppers.
